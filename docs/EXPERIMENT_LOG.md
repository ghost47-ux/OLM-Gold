## 2026-11-25 - synth90, 12 epochs (INVALID FOR EVALUATION)

- Data: 90 synthetic days, seed 7, 5,868 train / 1,253 validation / 1,254 test before the context-leak fix.
- Training: batch 64, 12 epochs, 1,104 optimizer steps, 1,238 s total, about 1.12 s/step and 57 samples/s.
- Final validation NLL: **-0.9841**; best checkpoint `synth90-20261125T161127Z-6a488bf6_best.pt`.
- Test: parent MAE 33.2383, child MAE 11.1758, CRPS 5.0588, coverage@50 0.7923, coverage@90 0.9926.
- **Why invalid:** H1/H4 context bars were selected by bar-open timestamp, so 100% of samples used an H1/H4 bar whose close was after the target start. This is lookahead leakage. The checkpoint and its report are retained only as a negative result and must not be promoted or quoted as forecast skill.

## 2026-11-26 - corrected point-in-time context, 1 epoch (SMOKE)

- Data: same 90 synthetic days after the fix; 5,857 train / 1,251 validation / 1,252 test. `leakage_check` reports **0 leaking H1 and H4 bars in every split**.
- Training: batch 64, 1 epoch, 92 steps, 131.08 s, 1.424 s/step, 44.9 samples/s, 4 PyTorch threads.
- Validation NLL: **5.3341** (parent 2.6126, child 2.6876). This is an underfit one-epoch model, not a converged result.
- Test: parent MAE 20.7253, child MAE 96.3164, CRPS 34.7597, coverage@50 0.9073, coverage@90 1.0000.
- Same test set, flat persistence: parent MAE **0.7034**, child MAE **0.4204**.
- **Conclusion:** the network trains and the leakage is fixed, but the one-epoch model is much worse than persistence and its intervals are over-wide. It is a pipeline smoke test, not a useful forecaster.

## 2026-11-26 - child-window correction, weekend audit, CPU batch benchmark (NO TRAINING)

### Child alignment and frozen split

- `_candidate_samples` now requires each target's three M5 children to sit at exactly `target + 0/5/10 minutes`. **26 malformed windows** were removed from train; previously their prices happened to reconstruct the M15 parent even though their timestamps did not form a 5-minute sequence.
- The chronological split boundary is computed from the pre-correction candidate timestamp universe, so the test period did not move. The final split is **56,709 train / 12,153 validation / 12,154 test**; train ends `2026-03-18 01:45Z`, validation ends `2026-07-22 18:45Z`, and the embargo is 60 minutes. **The 12,154-sample test partition is unchanged.**
- Preflight verifies exact child timestamps, parent timestamps, aggregation, H1 completeness and H4 completeness for every split. `leakage_check.ok = true`; leaking H1/H4 contexts are **0** in train, validation and test. The normalizer is fitted only on training M5 rows.

### Weekend structure

- Weekend M5 rows: **35,378** total (**18,738 Saturday + 16,640 Sunday**) out of 245,258. The first full Saturday session is **2025-04-26 00:00Z**; earlier weekends contain only a few bars per Sunday. The feed was expanded to full weekend sessions, not filled synthetically.
- Weekend bars are genuinely thinner. On 5-minute bars, flat-range share is **0.15% weekday vs 1.50% weekend**, mean range is **3.0862 vs 0.7242**, and mean range percent is **0.08510% vs 0.01802%**. On 15-minute bars, flat-range share is **0.08% vs 0.87%** and mean range is **5.7521 vs 1.2632**.
- The chronological split creates a real covariate shift: weekend share is **4,659 / 56,709 = 8.22% in train**, **3,449 / 12,153 = 28.38% in validation**, and **3,456 / 12,154 = 28.43% in test**. Only **329** training samples are from pre-expansion weekends; **4,330** are from full-session weekends.
- M5 daily coverage ranges from **11 to 288 bars**, median **276**. **119 days** have fewer than 144 bars; **10** of those are weekend days. No rows were deleted and no missing candles were invented.

### Day balancing

`day_balance: true` is now the default for training. It gives every UTC weekday present in the training split equal sampling mass by inverse frequency; absent weekdays get zero. Validation and test are never resampled. This reduces the train/evaluation weekday mix mismatch but does not remove the underlying regime difference; compare against `--no-day-balance` before claiming an effect.

### CPU batch-size benchmark

Single-run 20-step measurements, 2 warm-up steps, 8 PyTorch threads, `--no-update` (forward and backward, no optimizer step), on the 56,716-sample pre-freeze snapshot. The final frozen split has 56,709 training samples, a 0.01% change that leaves steps per epoch at **887**.

| Batch | Train ms/step | Samples/s | Forward ms/batch |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 16 | 262.59 | 60.93 | 78.70 |
| 32 | 551.02 | 58.07 | 128.88 |
| 64 | 985.78 | 64.92 | 311.96 |

A 10-step run gave **55.06 / 56.66 / 61.39 samples/s** for 16/32/64, and a 3-step run gave **59.67 / 53.07 / 51.29**. Batch 64 has the best median and halves the optimizer-step count, but the ranking reverses on the shortest run. Treat these as noisy single-machine measurements: batch 64 remains the configured default, and any real run should re-benchmark on its own host. A later run launched while an unrelated five-worker Python job occupied the CPU was discarded as contaminated.

The parent baseline is one-step; the child baseline is recursive M5. These are baseline diagnostics, not model results; the test split was not used to choose them and remains untouched.
